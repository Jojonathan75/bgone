Fixing hair, fur and difficult edges

Automatic background removal handles most images well, but some details are hard for any AI. Here is how to spot them and fix them in a minute.

Why some edges are difficult

The AI decides, pixel by pixel, what belongs to the subject. It struggles when the subject and the background look alike (a beige book on a beige table), when the edge is very thin (hair, fur, the strings of a racket) or when the object is see-through (glass, veils, smoke). In these areas the result may be cut too short, or keep a bit of the old background.

The tools, and when to use them

  • Eraser: removes leftover background. Use a soft brush (low hardness) along hair and fur, a hard brush on straight edges.
  • Restore brush: brings back parts that were removed by mistake. With Show the original faintly turned on, you can see exactly what you are painting back.
  • Smoothing: softens a jagged contour. One or two steps are usually enough; too much makes the subject look blurry.
  • Edge shrink / grow: shrinking by one or two pixels removes a thin halo of the old background, very common on dark subjects photographed on a light background.

Hair and fur

Do not try to paint every strand. Zoom out to normal size and judge the overall silhouette. Erase obvious patches of background between strands with a soft brush, then preview the result on the final background colour: a halo that is invisible on white can be obvious on dark blue. If the new background is similar in colour to the old one, small imperfections disappear naturally.

Shadows and reflections

A shadow on the floor is part of the background for the AI, but sometimes it stays as a faint grey area. Erase it if you want a floating object, or remove it and enable Drop shadow under the subject to get a clean, even shadow instead.

Glass and transparent objects

A glass bottle is mostly see-through, so a perfect result would keep it semi-transparent. Automatic tools usually make it either fully opaque or partly removed. For product photos, the simplest solution is often to restore the whole bottle as opaque and use a background close to the original one.

Undo is your friend

Every stroke can be undone with Ctrl+Z (Cmd+Z on Mac), and Back to the automatic cutout resets all your touch-ups. Experiment freely.

Transparent PNG: what it is and how to make one

A “transparent background” is not white and not a checkerboard: it is the absence of pixels. Here is how it works and how to avoid the most common mistakes.

What transparency means in an image

Each pixel of a digital image has three colour values (red, green, blue). Some formats add a fourth value called the alpha channel, which says how opaque the pixel is: 0 means invisible, 255 means fully visible, and anything in between is partly see-through. Good cutouts use these intermediate values on the edges, so hair or soft shadows blend naturally into any new background.

Which formats support it

  • PNG: full transparency, lossless. The safe choice for logos, stickers and cutouts.
  • WEBP: supports transparency with much smaller files. Perfect for websites, less supported by older software.
  • JPG: no transparency at all. If you save a cutout as JPG, the transparent area becomes a solid colour (usually white or black).
  • GIF: only all-or-nothing transparency, which gives rough edges. Avoid it for photos.

How to make a transparent PNG

  1. Open your image in {{brand}}. The background is removed automatically.
  2. In the Background tab, keep the checkerboard swatch selected: it means “transparent”.
  3. Click Download and choose PNG (or WEBP).

The fake checkerboard trap

Many images found online show a grey and white checkerboard but are not transparent at all: the checkerboard is printed into the pixels. You can spot it when the squares look slightly wavy, blurry, or move when you zoom. If you place such an image on a coloured background, the checkerboard appears. The fix is to cut it out again, as you would any other background.

Keeping transparency intact

  • Some messaging apps and social networks convert images to JPG and replace transparency with black or white. Send the file as a document to keep it.
  • When you edit the PNG in another program, export it again as PNG, not JPG.
  • Check the result by placing the image on a dark and a light background: edges should look clean on both.
